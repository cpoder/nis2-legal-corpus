## Scope: two regimes, the size test, the types in scope at any size, the classes against Article 3

<!-- meta: theme=scoping; type=reference; date=2026-11-12; surfaces=docs; guard=none -->

Five facts decide real cases. **The act has six doors, and the door decides
the regime**: public administration (a), state-controlled companies (b),
organisations identified by an authority (c), the businesses of Annexes 2 and
3 by size (d) or at any size (e), defence companies (f). **The size test is
the act's own since 2026**: medium-sized under the SME act, or 50 staff, or
turnover and balance sheet total above EUR 10 million. **A medium-sized
organisation of Annex 2 is essential**, where the directive makes it
important. **A state-controlled company is in scope whatever its sector, and
essential.** **The annexes list neither banking nor financial market
infrastructures**: an entity under DORA cannot be identified under (c), and
the incident handling chapter reaches it with deviations; the state-control
door (b) carries no such exception in its words.

test does not say whether the staff and the money of a group are added up, as

**The instant.** Both clocks run "a kiberbiztonsági incidensről való
tudomásszerzéstől", from becoming aware of the cybersecurity incident. The
directive's Hungarian text counts "a jelentős eseményről való
tudomásszerzéstől": the same word, tudomásszerzés. Hungary keeps the
directive's awareness anchor. Detecting is another word in both texts
("észlelése", § 4, point 47 of the act and the directive's definition of
incident handling), and neither uses it for the clocks.

**What the clock is attached to.** The directive counts from awareness of the
significant incident. The decree counts from awareness of the cybersecurity
incident, and the act decides which incidents are reported at all: every one
for the public side, those of § 66(2) for a business. § 66(2) does not use the
defined term of § 4, point 40. It names an effect in the present tense
("okoz", causes), where the directive's test and point 40(bb) also take an
incident that is capable of causing one ("képes okozni"). Read by its letter,
a business owes the 24 hours once the serious effect exists; no authority's
reading of that difference was found.

What the product did with it until 2026-11-12: Hungary had no incident
configuration and ran on DEFAULT, which never lets a recorded awareness time
move its clocks: they ran from detection, which can only be early, with the
final report a month after the notification, as § 77(1), point 5 of the
decree says. What it does since that day is in the section on the incident
configuration, below.

## Security measures: three security classes for each system, and the catalogue of 7/2024. (VI. 24.) MK rendelet

<!-- meta: theme=framework,supply-chain; type=reference; date=2026-11-12; surfaces=docs; guard=none -->

The act does not list the directive's ten themes. It makes each organisation
put each of its electronic information systems in one of three security
classes and apply the protective measures a ministerial decree assigns to
that class. Measured from the decree's text (table rows parsed cell by cell,
`mk-rendelet-7-2024.txt`):

- **Three annexes.** Annex 1 is the risk management framework and the way to
  classify (five numbered parts). Annex 2 is the catalogue of protective
  measures. Annex 3 is a catalogue of 47 threats, each marked for
  confidentiality, integrity or availability.
- **Annex 2: 19 families, 914 requirement groups.** A row is a requirement
  group (követelménycsoport) with its code, its name, its text and three
  columns, one per class (Alap, Jelentős, Magas), marked X or left with a dash.
- **385 groups are marked in at least one class, 529 in none.** The marks are
  cumulative, with no exception: 164 groups are marked in all three classes,
  138 in Jelentős and Magas, 83 in Magas alone. So a system of the basic
  class owes 164 groups, one of the significant class 302, one of the high
  class 385. (One dash is typed as an en dash: group 5.3, rewritten in
  December 2024, counted here with the 138.)
- **A group without a mark is a supplementary measure**, mandatory in no
  class (point 20.4, quoted below).
- **The requirements leave their parameters to the organisation**: how often,
  for whom and in which cases is to be set in its own policies (point 20.5).

**The digital types.** For an organisation the Commission's implementing
regulation addresses (the types of Art. 21(5) of the directive), § 6(2) puts
the directly applicable EU act first and the minister's decree second, for
what the EU act does not regulate. The act does not name the regulation and
exempts no type from the classes of § 10 or from the audit of § 16, whose
decree checks the catalogue's groups for every organisation. So a Hungarian
cloud or managed service provider is under both, in an order the texts state
and a division they do not draw.

**Where the catalogue comes from.** The decree names no model. The audit
decree does for its own method (NIST Special Publication 800-53A Revision 5,
quoted in the next section). Whether the catalogue itself follows the
catalogue that publication assesses was not checked against it: the family
names read like its families in translation, which is an impression, not a
finding.

## The cybersecurity audit: every two years, by a registered auditor, against the catalogue

<!-- meta: theme=framework; type=reference; date=2026-11-12; surfaces=docs; guard=none -->

The audit is how a business proves compliance. It is carried out by an
auditor on the SZTFH's register, against the catalogue of the MK decree,
and ends in two numbers. Measured from the audit decree's text
(`sztfh-rendelet-1-2025.txt`, tables parsed cell by cell):

- **Annex 6 lists 385 requirement groups: exactly the 385 the catalogue marks
  in at least one class**, none of the 529 supplementary ones (the two sets
  were compared by code, both ways). For each it says whether the group is
  checked for the organisation as a whole (132 groups, marked SZ) or system by
  system (253, marked EIR), which methods are mandatory (document review for
  all 385, an interview for 89, a test for 104), its type (133 Biztosító, 252
  Támogató) and whether it may be left out of the evaluation (59 may not).
- **Annex 7 breaks the groups down into 2,148 elementary requirements**
  (elemi követelmény), each with a reference code, under 384 of the 385
  groups: group 6.27 is in Annex 6 and has no row in Annex 7.
- **Annex 2 is a questionnaire of 25 questions** the organisation fills in
  for the auditor: turnover, number of systems per class, servers, sites,
  staff, endpoints, users.

## Departures from the directive, read in Hungarian

<!-- meta: theme=scoping,registration,incident,governance,framework,supply-chain,sanctions; type=reference; date=2026-11-12; surfaces=docs; guard=none -->

- **Two regimes by kind of organisation.** The public side, the
  state-controlled companies and the identified organisations answer to the
  national cybersecurity authority; the businesses of Annexes 2 and 3 to the
  SZTFH (§ 23(1)). The duties differ: the fee and the audit are owed by the
  businesses and by the state-controlled companies of the annexes (§ 7, § 16);
  only organisations under the national authority have the development of
  their systems approved (§ 13).
- **A medium-sized organisation of the high-criticality annex is essential**
  (§ 1(4)(f)), where the directive makes it important; so is a medium-sized
  non-qualified trust service provider.
- **State-controlled companies are in scope in any sector**, from 50 staff or
  the financial limb, and essential (§ 1(1)(b), (4)(b)). The directive has no
  such class.
- **The size test is the act's own since 2026-01-06**: medium-sized under the
  SME act, or 50 staff, or turnover and balance sheet total both above EUR 10
  million (§ 1(1)(d)), converted at the central bank's year-end rate
  (§ 1(1a)); entry on the first day of the following year and exit at the end
  of the second year (§ 8(6), (6a)).
- **Public administration is in at every level**: county and municipal
  offices, the courts, the prosecution, the central bank and the army are
  Annex 1 organisations, essential but for the offices of settlements of up to
  20,000 inhabitants (§ 1(4)(a), (5)(a)). Exclusions are by system (classified,
  operational), not by body (§ 3(1)).
- **No banking and no financial market infrastructures in the annexes**: an
  entity under DORA cannot be identified (§ 1(1)(c)), and the incident
  handling chapter applies to it with deviations (§ 1(10)(b), § 71).
- **National additions to the lists**: public transport operators,
  pharmaceutical wholesalers, holders of health reserves and blood stocks,
  cement, lime and plaster manufacturers, and domain name registrars as
  important entities (Annexes 2 and 3; § 1(1)(e)).
- **Identification reaches any organisation**, on thirteen grounds, with the
  burden of proof on the organisation (§ 1(6), § 26, § 27(4)).
- **Security classes for each system, and a catalogue of 914 requirement
  groups** in place of the directive's ten themes; the class is decided by
  the head on an impact analysis and reviewed every two years (§ 10; the MK
  decree).
- **A cybersecurity audit every two years** by an auditor on the authority's
  register, at a capped fee, with a score for each system and for the
  organisation, reported to the authority within 7 days (§ 16, § 21, § 22;
  1/2025. (I. 31.) SZTFH rendelet). Micro-enterprises in scope at any size are
  exempt.
- **A yearly supervision fee** paid by the supervised businesses (§ 7).
- **Registration within 30 days**, on the authority's form, with technical
  data: number of systems, public IP address, domain name, operators (§ 8(5);
  23/2023. (XII. 19.) SZTFH rendelet).
- **The clocks are attached to the cybersecurity incident, not to the
  significant incident** (Decree 418/2024, § 77(1)): the public side reports
  every incident, threat and near miss, an outage included (§ 66(1)); a
  business reports those of a serious effect and may report the others
  (§ 66(2), (3)).
- **The first act is a first notification (első bejelentés), not an early
  warning**, and the indicators of compromise are an act of their own
  (Decree 418/2024, § 77(1), points 1 and 2). A significant incident is also
  reported by telephone (the same decree, § 78(2)).
- **One recipient, the national incident handling centre**, which is the same
  body as the national authority of the public side; a report sent to an
  authority is passed on (§ 66; Decree 418/2024, § 19, § 65(1), § 78(3)).
- **One person answers**: the head of the organisation, not the management
  bodies (§ 6), with a person responsible for security appointed inside or
  outside (§ 11).
- **Training in hours**: 8 hours for a new head within a year, then 4 hours a
  year; 20 hours a year for the person responsible for security; by a
  registered adult-education provider, with a certificate (17/2025. (VII. 24.)
  EM rendelet).
- **An exercise every two years for every essential organisation**, with a
  report to the centre within 30 days (Decree 418/2024, § 10).
- **Fines by a table in forint** under the directive's ceilings, a fine on the
  head that only the national authority may impose, and a temporary ban of
  the head of an essential organisation (Decree 418/2024, § 42, Annex 3;
  § 30(3), (6)). Recorded as facts.

## The national baseline: a binding catalogue, graded by security class and audited (2026-11-12)

<!-- meta: theme=framework; type=reference; date=2026-11-12; surfaces=docs; guard=none -->

The catalogue of 7/2024. (VI. 24.) MK rendelet is a published and binding
national baseline that an organisation is assessed against. It is a
ministerial decree on the national law database, in force with no end date
and addressed to every organisation of the act (its § 1(2), § 2(1)). It
holds nineteen families and 914 requirement groups, each marked for the three
security classes: 164, 302 and 385 groups for the basic, significant and high
classes, and 529 supplementary groups that are mandatory in no class. And it
is what the audit checks: every two years (§ 16), by a decree that lists the
same 385 groups and breaks them into 2,148 elementary requirements. The
sections on the security measures and on the audit, above, quote each of
these.

Two facts of the texts qualify it. The class belongs to each system and not
to the organisation (§ 4, point 16; § 10(1), (2)): an organisation with one
basic and one high system owes 164 groups for the first and 385 for the
second, and only the organisation-level groups, 132 of the 385, are checked
at the highest class it holds (the audit decree's Annex 5, point 2.2.3.2).
And the catalogue is the minister's, while the audit, its method, its
auditors and its fee are the SZTFH's, whose review is open (previous
section).

- **The sentence on § 30(6) holds on both sides of 2027-01-01.** From that
  day point (b) names the court in place of the companies court (the adopted
  future states, first section). The English sentence says "a court"; the
  Hungarian one is the act's own, less the two bodies the authority turns to
  ("az illetékes hatóságnál", "a cégbíróságnál").
- **Both measures are initiated, not ordered.** The act's verb is
  "kezdeményezheti" for each: another authority suspends, a court bars. The
  pages keep that verb (`may initiate`, `ask a court`).
- **`Every report` is kept where the exception stands next to it.** The key
  fact and the answer on the recipient name the exception; the English page
  and the Hungarian page still say, in passing and once the exception is
  stated, that the national centre receives every report.
